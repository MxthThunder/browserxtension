India's Gaganyaan Mission Marks Historic Milestone as Crew Completes Orbital Docking Exercise

New Delhi, September 16 — In a landmark achievement for India's human spaceflight programme, the three-member Gaganyaan crew successfully completed their first autonomous orbital rendezvous and docking exercise on Tuesday, marking a critical milestone in the nation's journey toward a permanent space station.

Mission Commander Group Captain Vikram Nair guided the Orbital Module through a precision approach manoeuvre to within 300 metres of the uncrewed Service Vehicle, before handing control to automated systems for the final 50-metre docking sequence. The manoeuvre, executed at an altitude of 400 kilometres above the Indian Ocean, took approximately 47 minutes from initiation to hard dock.

"The crew performed flawlessly. This is exactly what two years of training at the Human Space Flight Centre in Bengaluru was designed to achieve." — ISRO Chairman Dr. V. Narayanan

The Indian Space Research Organisation confirmed that all systems aboard the Orbital Module — including the Environmental Control and Life Support System, the Attitude and Orbit Control System, and the Navigation, Guidance and Control suite — remained nominal throughout the exercise. Ground stations at ISTRAC Bengaluru, Port Blair, and the Brunei Tracking Station provided continuous coverage during the critical docking window.

Scientists at the Space Applications Centre in Ahmedabad and the Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre in Thiruvananthapuram monitored propulsion performance in real time, with telemetry confirming all thruster parameters were within expected limits. The mission's Flight Director confirmed that residual delta-V margin stands at approximately 342 metres per second, well above the 280 m/s minimum required for the deorbit and re-entry sequence scheduled for Day 7.

The docking exercise is the penultimate milestone before the mission's primary scientific objectives begin — a 72-hour programme of microgravity experiments covering materials science, protein crystallisation, and fundamental fluid dynamics research. Results from these experiments are expected to inform the design of India's proposed Bharatiya Antriksh Station, slated for initial module launch in 2030.
